Reflection

I chose The Landing because I was drawn to how our purpose in a space dictates what we actually notice within it. I took the photograph in the Teacher Education Building during our class photography activity, inspired by Georges Perec asking in Species of Spaces why we treat staircases merely as passageways rather than places to pause and live. Standing at the ground floor and aiming my camera straight up transformed an ordinary transit route into an abstract geometry of steel railings, window light, and concrete. The webpage keeps this photograph unchanged while three fictional voices inhabit a shared 4:17 p.m. moment. I wanted the contrast to come entirely from their differing relationships to the building, whether through a familiar routine, an uncertain visit, or a work shift, demonstrating that perception is guided by what we carry in our minds rather than the architecture alone.

Finding the right camera angle took several attempts; looking straight up flattened the vertical climb into overlapping planes, bringing the window, steel stringers, and pipes into a single frame while making the physical route disorienting. When I initially shared the concept with my TA, Kalysta Fern, the early drafts felt too uniform because all three voices were describing the same visual cues. To fix this, I created clearer distinctions in how each character thinks. The resident moves purely on muscle memory and domestic errands, completely ignoring the window. The visitor is caught in hesitation and social anxiety, unsure of where to go. The caretaker thinks in practical checklists and maintenance rhythms. Introducing deliberate blanks for details a character skips over made their selective attention feel believable.

I am very pleased with how the final webpage functions, particularly the Present mode and the detail comparison tool, which make the divergence between perspectives immediate and tactile. Building the interactive features allowed me to explore digital storytelling in a way a static essay could not, giving the viewer direct control over how they navigate the space. To ensure my TA could access the site reliably without login hurdles, I hosted the project on GitHub Pages, while also keeping an offline ZIP archive as a backup for grading. Developing this project helped me connect visual composition with creative writing, and if I were to expand it further, I would love to add ambient sound recordings from the stairwell to make each floor's atmosphere even more immersive.

Project notes

Mehul Garg / ENG 169

The Landing

I took this photograph in the Teacher Education Building during our Perec discussion activity. I chose this topic because I think different perspectives matter.

On the stairs

On p. 38, Perec writes, “We don’t think enough about staircases.” He asks, “We should learn to live more on staircases. But how?” Here, each narrator pauses between floors rather than simply passing through.

At the same time

On p. 40, Perec imagines an apartment building with its front removed, exposing its rooms simultaneously. This project places three people on different floors at 4:17 p.m. Their views overlap without becoming identical.

An inventory of attention

The lists of rooms, objects, and activities on pp. 41–43 inform the caretaker’s inventory voice. The resident’s omissions and the visitor’s questions give the other two voices different ways of attending to the same shaft.

Try the page

Change voices, open the five numbered details, or compare one detail across all three. A blank means that this person does not register that detail. “See without words” returns to the photograph alone.

Outside the course reading: The building project became Life A User’s Manual, which freezes an apartment building at a single moment (Godine’s publisher description).

Course reading

Perec, Georges. Species of Spaces and Other Pieces. Edited with an introduction and translated by John Sturrock, Penguin, Penguin Twentieth-Century Classics, pp. 38, 40–43. Course PDF.
